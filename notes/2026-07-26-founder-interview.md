# Founder interview — own wedding experience (2026-07-26)

Source: alignment conversation at project kickoff. The founder is recently
married (Saturday 18:00 ceremony, Shilla Hotel Dynasty Hall), so their own
wedding serves as the first primary-data user interview.

## Day-of timeline (groom's view)

- Fri: final fitting at tailor shop, evening check-in at the hotel.
- Sat AM: wife to hotel massage shop; groom hails a taxi to the makeup shop
  (~12:00). Both sets of parents got makeup at the same shop.
- ~15:00: pre-booked van to the hotel. Van driver kept calling — stressful.
- At hotel: ceremony photos, rehearsal, then guest reception 17:00–18:00
  (the single most hectic window).
- 18:00 ceremony, parts 1–2, settlement/정산, farewells to parents, home.
- On the way home: thank-you KakaoTalk messages to guests.

## Incidents (all three at transition moments)

1. Departing for makeup shop — no taxi pre-booked → scramble.
2. Departing the makeup shop — necktie left in the car.
3. Departing the hotel after the ceremony — some luggage left behind;
   went back alone to retrieve it.

Pattern: failures cluster at "leaving one place for the next," not at random
points. A checklist keyed to transition moments matches the actual failure
mode better than a flat to-do list.

## Phone availability (core product constraint)

- Phone usable and checked freely during the prep morning through ~15:00.
- From rehearsal onward (~2–3h before ceremony, case-by-case) the phone was
  effectively unusable; it sat in the bride's waiting room and was carried
  around by a friend (가방순이) taking photos.
- During the blackout window, coordination happened through humans: a
  last-minute request to reorder the congratulatory song (축가) was relayed
  by the hotel 지배인 physically walking between parties.
- Founder's judgment on alerts: **would have seen them** during the prep
  window. Cadence that would work: once in the morning + a few mid-day
  pings at transition points. Not a stream.

## Wife's experience

- Day-of itself: not the hard part for her.
- The preparation phase was where she suffered: recurring content-heavy
  vendor meetings (e.g. flower meeting), and above all **headcount
  estimation and seat assignment** (hotel weddings require assigned
  seating).
- RSVP pain: their mobile-invitation (모바일 청첩장) vendor delivered RSVPs
  by email, which then had to be manually re-entered into their own
  tracking — double bookkeeping.

## Market signal

Shilla Hotel is a best-case environment — planner, 지배인, managers layered
everywhere — and it was still this chaotic. At ordinary wedding halls or
small weddings without that human buffer, the couple absorbs the 지배인
role themselves.
